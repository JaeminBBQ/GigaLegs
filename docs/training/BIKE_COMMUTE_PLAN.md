# Bike Commute Plan: Five Ridges → South Meadows

## The route (measured 2026-10-03 from OpenStreetMap routing + USGS elevation; street-level details are in `athlete/PROFILE.md`)
- **Start:** Five Ridges, north Sparks (off Pyramid Way / Sparks Blvd area).
- **Destination:** work in South Meadows (exact address is in `athlete/PROFILE.md`, which is private).
- **Recommended path (D18):** Sparks Blvd (bike lanes on the north half) → cross I-80 → **Erica Greif Memorial Bikeway**, a separated path running alongside Veterans Pkwy for about 4 mi → South Meadows Pkwy (bike lanes) → work.
  - **Use the bikeway, not the Veterans roadway.** OpenStreetMap shows the roadway as 55 mph and 3 lanes with no bike lane where the path runs beside it. The last ~1 mi on Veterans before South Meadows Pkwy has no path or tagged lane (to verify on the recon ride).
  - **Sparks Blvd has an on-road bike lane the whole way** once you leave the neighborhood. The athlete checked this on Street View (2026-10-04); OSM was missing tags on the southern part. The care point is the **I-80 interchange**: cars crossing the bike lane to reach the on-ramps. Look over your shoulder, signal, and cross the ramp lanes at a right angle; don't ride the merge.
  - OSM data is incomplete in places (about 2.5 mi of the route had no tags), so the weekend recon ride is the final word. Note anything that feels sketchy, and Claude will re-route around it.
- **Distance:** **~16–17 mi one way, ~33 mi round trip** (depending on the exact path). At an easy pace that's roughly 70–90 min in and 80–100 min home.
- **Elevation:** ~230 ft of climbing inbound and **~615 ft homebound**. The profile is lopsided:
  - Inbound: a fast descent of ~370 ft in the first ~1.7 mi (cold in winter mornings; dress for wind chill and check your brakes), then nearly flat.
  - Homebound: ~15 flat-ish miles, then **the last ~1.7 mi climbs ~370 ft, peaking around 7%.** That final climb is the "boss" of the commute. Ride it in your easiest gears, seated, at conversation pace.
  - Truckee Meadows afternoon winds are often from the west/southwest, so the ride home is the hard leg.
- **Safety recon to do on a weekend, by bike, before the first commute:** the I-80 crossing on Sparks Blvd, where you get on and off the Greif bikeway, the last mile on Veterans to South Meadows Pkwy, and where you'll lock up/change at work. Ride with lights front and rear (daylight too; mandatory once it gets dark early in winter).

## Season reality check
Program Weeks 1–10 run from late October to early January, so Stages 3–5 land in winter: cold mornings (often below freezing), early sunsets, and occasional snow or ice. Plan for this:
- Ride one way in only in daylight or with strong lights. Don't ride on ice; take the car or bus that day, with no streak penalty.
- Expect regular round-trip commuting to really start in late winter or spring. Bike-plan stages are gated on fitness *and* conditions, so a weather-delayed stage isn't a failure.
- Practice the final climb on its own on weekend rides (ride down and back up).

## Progression stages
Advance a stage when you've completed its target for **2 consecutive weeks** without it hurting your lifting (readiness soreness ≤ 5, lifting RPE on target). All riding is Zone 1–2 unless stated.

| Stage | Weekly target | Longest ride | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Base** (during on-ramp) | 2–3 rides, 60–120 min total | 45 min | Flat and easy: Sparks Marina, Truckee River path. Get bike fit/saddle comfortable. |
| **2. Build** | 3 rides, 2–3 h total | 60–75 min (~12–15 mi) | Include some of the real route. Practice the climb home at easy effort. |
| **3. Recon + one-way** | 1 one-way commute (downhill morning ride in) + 2 easy rides | One-way commute | Get home by RTC bus (bike rack), car, or ride the next morning. Full route recon done on a weekend first. |
| **4. First round trips** | 1 round trip + 1 one-way | Round trip | Ride home is Zone 2 — it's long; go slow. Eat before the ride home. |
| **5. Regular commuter** | 2–3 round trips | Round trip | Stay here. Cut to ≤ 2 round trips/week in the last 2–3 weeks of the deadlift program (peak). |

Rough timeline: Stage 5 in ~10–14 weeks if nothing stalls. Weekly ride time should grow by roughly ≤ 15–20%.

## Interaction with lifting (summary — full rules in METHODOLOGY §5)
- Commute days ideally = non-lifting days.
- No hard riding the day before heavy squat/deadlift.
- If leg soreness ≥ 7, swap a commute for the bus/car — no penalty to streaks.
- Fuel the ride: a round trip of this length is a big energy cost. Under-eating will stall squat and deadlift progress.

## Bike basics (you have a geared bike; this is all you need to know to start)
- **Get a tune-up first.** Before Stage 2, take the bike to a local shop and ask for a basic tune-up and a saddle-height check. Tell them you're going to commute about 33 mi round trip with a steep finish, and ask whether your tires suit road riding.
- **Gears:** the left shifter changes the front rings (big jumps), and the right shifter changes the rear cogs (small steps).
  - Climbing / the ride home: small front ring + bigger rear cogs = easy pedaling.
  - Flats and downhill: bigger front ring + smaller rear cogs.
  - Shift *before* a hill, while still pedaling but easing off the pressure. Never shift under heavy force.
  - Avoid "cross-chaining" (big front + biggest rear, or small front + smallest rear).
  - Aim for a smooth ~75–90 pedal revolutions per minute. Spinning easy gears saves your legs for squats; grinding hard gears doesn't.
- **Saddle height:** with your heel on the pedal at the bottom of the stroke, your leg is almost straight. In normal pedaling, your knee stays slightly bent at the bottom.
- **Weekly 2-minute check:** tire pressure (the range is printed on the tire sidewall), brakes grab firmly, chain isn't dry or squeaky (lube it every ~100–150 mi).
- **Learn to fix a flat** once at home before you rely on the bike for commuting. A YouTube video and one practice run is enough.

## Kit checklist
Helmet, front + rear lights, lock, flat kit (tube, levers, mini pump/CO2), water, a snack for the ride home, layers (Reno mornings are cold Oct–Apr), change of clothes or a plan to change at work.
